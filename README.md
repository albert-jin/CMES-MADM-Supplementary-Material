# Supplementary Material for the Multi Agent Cooperative Decision Making Survey

## Scope and evidence basis

This supplement describes the literature search, selection criteria, and organization used in the survey. The search covered literature available through August 2026. The original search and screening records are currently unavailable; the procedure below follows the authors' account and the methodology recorded in the submitted manuscript.

## Supplementary Material S1 Search and selection

Searches covered Google Scholar, arXiv, IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect, OpenReview, and the ACL Anthology. Backward and forward citation searching from surveys, benchmarks, and environment papers supplemented these sources. The emphasis was on publications from 2017 onward, with earlier work retained for relevant foundations and historical examples.

Four query families guided selection: multi-agent decision-making; MARL and coordination; LLM-based multi-agent systems; and environments, benchmarks, and applications. Representative terms included multi-agent decision-making, multi-agent reinforcement learning, centralized training decentralized execution, value decomposition, multi-agent communication, large language model agent, coordination, simulation environment, autonomous driving, UAV, robotics, and social simulation. Combinations were adapted to the platform interface. These terms describe the search concepts, not a recovered platform-by-platform search log.

Selection involved identification and deduplication, title-and-abstract screening, and full-text scope checking. Eligible publications or technical preprints addressed a multi-agent method, system, environment, benchmark, or application and provided sufficient detail for comparison. Duplicate or superseded versions, reports without adequate technical content, and works with only a peripheral multi-agent component were excluded from the main synthesis. Identifiers, normalized titles, authors, and years were used to reconcile versions.

Cooperative teams are central. Competitive and mixed-motive settings are included when within-team coordination or negotiation is relevant. Single-agent methods and general-purpose tools provide background where needed. Exact query logs, per-stage counts, and reviewer-level screening decisions cannot currently be recovered.

## Supplementary Material S3 Classification and comparison fields

The synthesis considers publication year, decision-making mechanism, training and execution information, coordination channel, task or environment, observation and action interfaces, feedback, and application role. The primary mechanism determines the method category; secondary mechanisms identify hybrids. Benchmarks, frameworks, and algorithms are treated as distinct roles.

Software documentation supports interface descriptions. Research papers support methodological and empirical claims. Simulation, physical demonstration, and deployment evidence are distinguished. Experiments with different tasks and budgets are not pooled into a numerical ranking, and formal study-quality or risk-of-bias scores are not assigned.

## Supplementary Material S4 Bibliographic updates

The duplicate MADDPG references are consolidated. Irrelevant sentiment-analysis, metonymy-resolution, and agricultural-text-classification citations have been removed from the manuscript. CrewAI and LangGraph now cite their official documentation. Other corrections concern publication records, author names, method attribution, and benchmark names.

The utility-theory survey now cites Qin Yang's March 2026 version, Utility Theory based Cognitive Modeling in the Application of Robotics: A Survey, at https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09445v3. The satellite study now cites Cameron Mehlman and Gregory Falco's April 2026 version, Satellite Chasers: Divergent Adversarial Reinforcement Learning to Engage Intelligent Adversaries on Orbit, at https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17443v2. Unverified page ranges for the Miki WAC 2010 and Zhang ASCC 2019 records have been removed.

Fifteen 2025–2026 primary studies supplement the earlier coverage. Seven address MARL coordination: Autonomous Partner Selection, Cross-Environment Cooperation, ExpoComm, AsynCoMARL, communication-constrained priors, O-MAPL and OMSD. Five address LLM-agent methods and interaction strategies: G-Designer, CortexDebate, EIB-Learner, activation exchange, and the study of governance, participation and context management by Wang et al. Three introduce collaboration benchmarks: MultiAgentBench, Collab-Overcooked and PAC-Bench. All fall within the August 2026 coverage period. These studies are discussed in Sections 3.4 and 4.2, with representative entries in Tables 4, 6 and 8. S5 follows the second-round manuscript's reference order; the main bibliography supplies full publication details and links.
